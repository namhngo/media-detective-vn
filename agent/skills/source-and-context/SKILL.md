# Source and context

## Procedure

1. Preserve the supplied asset or URL as the submitted version. Do not assume a
   crop, screenshot, compressed upload, or repost is the original publication.
2. Check what the visible material actually establishes: publisher or account,
   publication date, caption, claimed location, speaker, organization, and
   event. List the fields that are absent or cannot be confirmed.
3. Ask for or search for the earliest attributable appearance. A genuine image
   can still be misleading when it is old, mislocated, cropped, or given a
   false caption.
4. Recommend the smallest fitting verification action: open the original post,
   look for the full video, reverse-image search a still, compare official
   announcements, or find independent reporting from the claimed place and time.
5. Treat missing captions, context, or source links as a verification gap, not
   proof of fabrication. State the gap precisely.

## Provenance boundaries

- If Content Credentials are supplied and validated by a tool, describe the
  signed provenance record, signer, and recorded actions accurately.
- A valid provenance record does not prove that the depicted event happened or
  that its caption is fair.
- Content Credentials and ordinary metadata can be absent after screenshots,
  compression, copying, or platform uploads. Their absence is not proof that
  media is AI-generated or manipulated.
- Do not claim to have performed a reverse-image search or provenance validation
  unless a tool result was actually supplied.
