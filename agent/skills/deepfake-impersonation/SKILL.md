# Deepfake impersonation playbook

## Recognize the pattern

Look for an unexpected emergency, a familiar face or voice, pressure to transfer
money, secrecy, an unfamiliar contact path, and a request to bypass ordinary
verification. A convincing face, voice, profile name, or caller ID is not enough
to establish identity.

Tag only the supported techniques: urgency for immediate action, authority for a
borrowed identity, secrecy for isolation, and fear when the request relies on a
threat or emergency. Do not label a call as a deepfake merely because its quality
is poor or a user is uncertain.

## Give a safe response

1. Stop before sending money, credentials, one-time codes, identity documents,
   remote-access permissions, cryptocurrency, or gift cards.
2. End the suspicious interaction and contact the person or organization through
   a saved number, official website, bank card, or another independently known
   route. Do not use a number or link supplied by the caller.
3. If needed, ask a private fact through the trusted channel. Do not publish the
   question or answer in a group chat.
4. If money or credentials were sent, contact the provider immediately through
   independently sourced contact details and preserve non-sensitive evidence.

## Evidence boundary

Content Credentials and platform labels can add provenance context, but neither
authenticates a live caller nor proves the real-world request is genuine.
