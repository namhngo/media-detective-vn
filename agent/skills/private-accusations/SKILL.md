# Person-targeting accusations

## Immediate safeguards

1. Enter heightened-harm mode for allegations, shaming, sexualized content, or
   calls to act against a person. Do not treat the allegation as established
   merely because it is viral or names a recognizable person.
2. Distinguish public figures acting in public roles and already-public legal
   proceedings from allegations about private people. A public name may be used
   only when necessary to verify a public-interest claim.
3. Do not request, retain, compare, or redistribute intimate imagery or alleged
   proof. For private people, keep the structured claim anonymous and minimal;
   never retain handles, schools, workplaces, addresses, contact details, or
   other unnecessary identifiers.
4. Never recommend contacting, confronting, exposing, or investigating the
   targeted person. Verification should use records and responsible reporting,
   not crowdsourced pursuit.
5. Use Google Fact Check once for a minimally identifying factual allegation.
   For a public figure or public proceeding, include only the public name and
   role needed to distinguish the claim. For a private person, remove
   nonessential identifiers. Never send intimate information, contact details,
   a precise home location, school, workplace, or private account identifier.
6. Use Exa only when a public figure's public role is material or the event is
   already responsibly reported as a public-interest matter. Search the event,
   not local gossip, and never try to reconstruct hidden identifiers.

## Analysis and next action

- Identify fabrication, decontextualization, emotional bait, bandwagon pressure,
  and character attacks when the submitted material supports them.
- State what reliable evidence supports, what conflicts, and what remains
  unverified. Virality is not evidence, and sharing an unsupported accusation
  can cause irreversible harm even when a claim is later withdrawn.
- Recommend not sharing, using the platform's reporting route, and preserving
  only non-sensitive report details when a report is needed.
- For intimate-image abuse, suggest the appropriate specialist reporting route
  without promising removal or a legal outcome. StopNCII is for adults; Take It
  Down is for images taken before age 18. Never ask a person to download or
  re-share an image just to make a report.
